# Verification of reference solutions

`benchfea.verify` decides whether a reference solution (the results of a model, or the mechanism
diagnosis of an unstable model) may be published as **verified**. Results of actual runs are in
[VERIFICATION_REPORT.md](VERIFICATION_REPORT.md) (generated by `tools/build_verification_report.py`).

## 1. What "verified" means — and what it does not

> **Verified** means only: the reference solution passed every tier listed as applicable in its record,
> at the tolerances stated in it.

It is a statement about the agreement of independent computations. It does not mean that anybody has
reviewed, approved or certified the result, that the model represents a real structure well, or that the
design is adequate. Every record carries this sentence (`meaning`).

Rules of the pipeline:

* **Every applicable tier must pass.** There is no vote. One applicable tier that is not passed — because
  two computations differ, because fewer than two independent solvers can represent the model, because a
  check could not be built or was not run — makes the record `quarantined`, with the reason.
* **The record lists the tiers passed**, the largest differences found and the tolerances used.
* **Tolerances are written down once** (`benchfea.verify.POLICY`, section 5) and are the same for every
  model and problem. A case that misses is quarantined with a diagnosis; no tolerance is changed, no case
  dropped and no model special-cased to make a check pass.
* **A refusal is not a result.** A model the engine refuses (invalid, unsupported) has no reference
  solution: status `refused`.

```python
from benchfea.verify import verify_model, verify_problem, verify_library
rec = verify_model("frame.json", design=False, tiers="all", budget=None)   # path, dict or Document
rec.status            # "verified" | "quarantined" | "refused"
rec.kind              # "results" | "diagnosis" (unstable model)
rec.tiers["C"]        # TierResult: applies, passed, summary, largest, tolerances, details, category
rec.tiers_passed, rec.quarantine, rec.reference, rec.design_reference, rec.timings, rec.versions
rec.to_dict()         # JSON data; to_dict(volatile=False) has no timings and is deterministic
```

Command line: `benchfea verify FILE [--design] [--tiers ACDE] [--budget S] [--json] [-o record.json]`,
`benchfea verify PROBLEM_DIR`, `benchfea verify --all`. Exit code 0 verified / every active problem
passed; 5 quarantined or an active problem fails; 1 refused; 2 usage.

`quarantine.category` says what kind of reason it is (the most serious one found):

| category | meaning |
|---|---|
| `disagreement` | two computations of the same quantity differ by more than the tolerance |
| `error` | an equivalence check that applies could not be built or solved |
| `not-verifiable` | this kind of reference solution cannot be verified here (section 4) |
| `not-covered` | a design limit state used lacks tier-F coverage |
| `not-representable` | fewer than two foreign solvers can represent the model: it is **not cross-checked and therefore not publishable as verified** |
| `not-run` | a tier was excluded by the caller or stopped by the time budget |

## 2. The tiers

| tier | what | applies to |
|---|---|---|
| A | closed form: the independent ODE oracle (`benchfea.oracle`), and targets derived here | models the oracle can represent; registered tier-A problems |
| B | published problems reproduced, each cited | registered tier-B problems (`verify_problem`) |
| C | the same model solved by two independent solvers (OpenSeesPy, PyNite) | every model |
| D | invariants of every solve | every model |
| E | equivalence checks of the modelling features | every model (the checks whose feature is present) |
| F | coverage of the design limit states | when design results are part of the reference (`design=True`) |

### Tier A — ODE oracle / derived here

`benchfea.oracle` integrates the member differential equations directly (`scipy.integrate.solve_bvp`): no
stiffness matrix, shape function or fixed-end force is shared with the engine
([CROSSCHECK.md](CROSSCHECK.md) section 2). The tier applies automatically when the model is **one
straight line of frame members parallel to a global axis** (single member, continuous beam, beam-column):
supports and support springs, imposed displacements, every load type, bending releases that form an
internal hinge or a released end at a rotation-fixed end node, first order with or without shear
deformation, second order without shear deformation and with a piecewise constant axial force, stiffness
reduction with a given τ_b. The engine is compared with the oracle at every node (displacements,
reactions) and at every result station (six forces, three displacements). A combination the oracle cannot
represent is left to the other tiers and named in the record; buckling factors are not covered by the
automatic comparison (they are covered by registered problems).

Targets of a registered tier-A problem are "derived here": computed in this repository (by the oracle, or
by a numerically solved characteristic equation) with the script kept in `verification/derive/`. They are
never attributed to a book.

### Tier B — published problems

A problem of the library with `"tier": "B"` reproduces a published example. The problem file cites the
source (work, edition, example / figure / equation number, pages), gives the data that define the problem
and the target numbers; no text, table or figure of a source is in the repository. Targets are compared
at the tolerance of section 5. For a model that is not a registered problem the tier does not apply.

The `basis` of a problem says what its targets are, and the report counts them separately (section 1.1 of
the report):

* `published` — every target is a number printed in the source.
* `recomputed-from-published-statement` — only the problem STATEMENT is published (the vendor verification
  pages cite textbooks that were not opened; some examples of the textbook give a closed-form expression
  instead of numbers). The targets are computed in this repository by means that share no code with the
  engine (the ODE oracle, a separate small frame program, the source's closed-form expression evaluated
  here), at 10⁻⁶. **Such a problem is not a reproduction of a published number**: it is evidence of the
  same kind as tier A (two computations made by this project). The values the source does print are added
  as targets at their printed precision; those that do not reproduce are kept as a separate problem
  `<id>-vendor-printed`, quarantined with a diagnosis — they are not dropped, and the split is visible in
  the report.
* A printed number the model was calibrated on (the source gives no section properties) is marked
  "reproduced by construction, not evidence" in its `precisionNote` and counted separately.

### Tier C — two independent solvers

`benchfea.crosscheck` translates the model to OpenSeesPy and to PyNite and keeps a fidelity record per
feature (`exact` / `approximate` with an error bound computed from runs of the foreign solver alone /
`unsupported`). A combination is cross-checked only if **at least two** foreign solvers represent it —
exactly, or by a converged approximation whose estimated error is at most `maxApproximation` — and they
agree with the engine and with each other within `relative` plus the estimated errors of the two results
compared. This is the rule of `benchfea.crosscheck.cross_check`; the numbers of the policy are unchanged.

If fewer than two solvers can represent the model the tier reads "not passed (only N solver(s) can
represent: reasons)", category `not-representable`. **Such a model is not publishable as verified**, even
when tier A passes. With the solvers available this concerns in particular: shear deformation (PyNite has
none; OpenSees only first order), partial axial / shear / torsion releases (PyNite), rigid offsets in a
second-order analysis, **rigid links and diaphragms in a second-order analysis** (PyNite's stiff-member
emulation solves the rigid-body problem, the engine and OpenSees the linear constraint: CROSSCHECK.md
3.5), second-order analyses whose foreign discretisation error bound exceeds the limit, and **linear
buckling** (neither translator computes buckling factors).

Substitutions (each use is written into the record under `substitutions`; none is silent). They concern
quantities the engine derives from its own solution and that a foreign solver cannot recompute; in each
case the foreign model is the same mathematical problem:

* *Notional loads*: recomputed here from the load definitions (no solver code), compared with the loads
  the engine recorded (10⁻⁹), then given to the foreign solvers as nodal loads.
* *Computed τ_b*: AISC 360-22 Eq. C2-2a/b re-evaluated here from the engine's station axial forces and
  compared with the τ_b the engine used; the foreign solvers run with these values. Agreement of their
  member forces with the engine's closes the loop.
* *Tension-/compression-only members*: the foreign solvers get the linear model of the engine's final
  active set (active members as truss members, slack members removed); then, from the **foreign**
  results, every active member must carry a force of its permitted sign and every slack member must be
  strained in its forbidden sense. These conditions characterise the solution of the one-sided problem
  exactly. Member loads of a slack member (its self weight, for example) reach its end nodes as the
  reactions of a simply supported beam (THEORY 13); they are computed here from the load definitions
  with the force-method routine of `crosscheck.fef` (transverse forces and the axial component by the
  lever rule, a point moment as a couple, carried over a rigid offset with its moment) and given to the
  foreign solvers as nodal loads (`tier_c.slack_member_loads`; recorded as `slackMembersWithLoads`).
  Tier D checks the same rule on the engine's own results: the end forces of a loaded slack member
  must be those reactions, and its station forces must follow from them by statics.

What is *not* done: a model with constraints or offsets is never cross-checked through a different
structure (for example with the constraints replaced by members). That comparison exists, but as an
equivalence check of tier E with a convergence series, not as a foreign solve.

*Extra evidence* (`extra_evidence=True`, `--extra-evidence`): the independent "tableau" solver of
`tests/independent` (a different method, written for the review of the engine) is run as well and its
largest difference is recorded under tier C. It is extra evidence only: it is not one of the two
open-source solvers the tier requires and it never changes the status.

#### Second-order cross-checks: the foreign error bound

Both foreign routes discretise the beam-column problem (CROSSCHECK.md 3.4). The allowed difference
between two results is `relative` plus the error bounds of the two; the engine's bound is zero. The
bound of a foreign solver comes from **three refinement levels of that solver** and nothing else
(CROSSCHECK.md 1.3): the last level-to-level difference, enlarged when the observed contraction is
worse than first order or better than the method can have, and *no estimate* — the solver is then not
eligible — when the levels do not contract. `estimateSafety` stays 1.0: the bound is used as it is.

This replaces the leading-order Richardson estimate, which equalled the error itself when the engine
was exact and understated it when a first-order term was present; correct models then missed tier C by
less than 1 % up to a factor 2 (`derived/beam-column-02`: 8.010·10⁻⁴ against an allowed 8.008·10⁻⁴).
The cause of the first-order term, the acceptance table (estimate ≥ true error against the ODE oracle
and the tableau solver on 30 models; est/true = 3 for OpenSees) and the test that an engine result
falsified by 10⁻³ is still rejected are in CROSSCHECK.md 1.3 and 3.4.

What the allowed difference means for a verified second-order record: agreement with OpenSees within
about three times the OpenSees discretisation error at 32 elements per member (10⁻⁵…10⁻³), and with
PyNite within its bound (10⁻⁶…10⁻³). The record lists both bounds next to the differences found.

### Tier D — invariants

Stored by the solver with every combination and collected here (definitions and tolerances:
[THEORY.md](THEORY.md) 8, 15): stiffness symmetry, zero energy under rigid-body motion, positive
definiteness after supports, linear solve residual, global force and moment equilibrium, joint
equilibrium, member equilibrium, member compatibility, work = strain energy, and for second order member
equilibrium on the deformed axis. A missing required invariant or a combination that did not converge
fails the tier. The tier adds two runs on the first-order operator of the model: **superposition**
(factored sum of the separately solved load cases = the combination solved directly) and **reciprocity**
(Maxwell–Betti on a deterministic selection of DOF pairs). Both are properties of a linear system; with
tension-/compression-only members they are not run (recorded).

The stored invariants are computed by the solver during the solve. Two further parts are computed by the
verification layer **from the finished reference solution**, without solver code
(`benchfea.verify.stations`), because tier C compares node displacements, reactions and member end forces
only — not the values at the result stations between the member ends, from which a largest span moment is
read:

* **station statics** — at every result station the six internal forces are recomputed from the station
  forces at the member start and the member loads taken from the load definitions (free body on the
  undeformed axis; in a second-order combination the axial forces act with the lever of the stored
  deflections, `dMz/dx = −Vy + N v′`); the station forces at the two ends must equal the end forces.
  Tolerance 10⁻⁹ (linear analysis), 10⁻⁶ (iterated analysis).
* **station integration** — the member differential equations (THEORY.md 1.1, 10.2) are integrated between
  the first and the last station as initial value problems (`scipy.integrate.solve_ivp`; the start rotation
  from the deflection of the last station) and compared at every interior station: displacement of the
  axis, bending moments, shears; the axial displacement from `u′ = N/EA`. The displacement of the member
  axis at each end must equal the node displacement carried over the rigid offset. Tolerance 10⁻⁶, as for the ODE
  oracle of tier A.

At a point load the results hold a station for the limit from the left and one for the limit from the
right; a regular station that falls on the load within round-off of its position adds a third, whose side
is decided by an ulp. A station at a point load is therefore compared with both limits and the nearer
one counts, and the stations of the point together must show both (a jump that is missing in the results
fails). Tier A compares such a station with the nearer limit of the oracle in the same way.

A station value that is wrong in the results therefore fails tier D even when every node value is right
(regression tests: `tests/test_verify_stations.py`). Scales: the largest station force, the largest
station moment and the largest member-axis displacement of the combination (a quantity that is zero in
the whole model, such as the moments of a truss, gets a floor of 10⁻⁶ × the other scale × the model size,
so that round-off is not turned into a ratio).

### Tier E — equivalence checks of the modelling features

Each check rewrites the input document into a model that must give the same answer by a different route
through the engine, solves it and compares on the original nodes and members. Applied automatically when
the feature is present:

| check | applies when | equivalent model | tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| `designVariables` | always | every K factor, unbraced length, C_b, C_m, method changed | every analysis result **bitwise identical** |
| `memberSplit` | a frame member can be cut | members cut in two at a result station (loads, releases, offsets carried over) | 10⁻⁸ first order, 10⁻⁶ iterated analyses |
| `rigidOffsets` | rigid end offsets | offset replaced by an added node and a stub member, stiffness ×10 per level | limit vs engine 10⁻⁵ |
| `constraints` | rigid links / diaphragms | constraint replaced by stiff members (diaphragm links released out of plane) | limit vs engine 10⁻⁵ |
| `fullReleases` | full releases on frame members | release replaced by a connection spring going to zero | limit vs engine 10⁻⁵ |
| `fixedEnds` | released members with a rigid bending connection | rigid connection replaced by a spring going to infinity | limit vs engine 10⁻⁵ |

A stiffness series is adaptive: the stiffness changes by a factor 10 per level until the answer stops
changing in the expected way (observed order of convergence in [0.5, 1.5]; a level that leaves that range
marks the round-off floor and is discarded; at most 8 levels). The stopping rule never looks at the
engine's result. The limit is the Richardson extrapolation of the last three levels (the error of a stiff
or soft substitute is a e + b e² + …), or the last level when the series has stopped changing; it is
compared with the engine. A series that neither converges at the expected order nor stops changing has
no limit and the check is not passed. A check that applies but cannot be constructed or solved is **not
passed**.

Stated limits of these checks: `constraints` is run on the first-order counterpart of second-order
combinations (the engine's rigid constraints carry no geometric stiffness, a stiff member does); a
stiffness series compares the combination results, not buckling factors; `memberSplit` cuts at most 40
members and leaves truss-type members alone (an added joint on a pin-ended member is a mechanism), as
well as loaded members when the model uses notional loads or a computed τ_b (those rules are defined per
analysis member). The cut is made at an interior result station that is neither at a point load nor
closer than 10⁻³ of the member length to a point load or to the end of a partial line load: a cut next
to a load position leaves a sliver between the new joint and the load, and the stiffness contrast of
that sliver makes the *substitute* ill-conditioned (measured: a point load 10⁻⁵ L beside the cut is
refused as "almost a mechanism" — Ziemian frame 2). A member without such a station is skipped with
that reason. The comparison tolerance is not affected.

### Tier F — design checks

Applies when design results are part of the reference solution. Every limit state **used** must have
coverage in the test suite of both kinds: **(a)** a hand calculation worked from AISC 360-22 and **(b)** a
reproduction of a printed value of the AISC Design Examples v16.0. The registry is machine readable:
`src/benchfea/verify/design_coverage.json`, generated from the design tests by
`tools/build_design_coverage.py` (`--check` tells whether it is up to date). It is keyed by the items of
a check's clause — every equation and table cited (`Eq. E3-2`, `Table E7.1`), a section cited without an
equation (`Sec. E2`), the cases of Tables B4.1a/b used for the classification (`Table B4.1b Case 15`); the
clause lists the branch actually taken, so the items identify the limit state at the level of the
equations evaluated.

How the registry is derived (nothing is typed by hand): the design tests are run with hooks.
(a) = the item appears in a check with a result produced inside a `test_hand_*` test (those tests write
the Specification's arithmetic out in the test body) **and the test's own code read the nominal or
available strength or the utilisation of that check** (for a limit check: the measured value); a check the
engine merely produced on the way — the other limit states of the same member — earns no credit, and a
hand test that reads only an intermediate value of a check is listed under `aDetail` and does not count.
One equation (F1-1, C_b) has its hand calculation on the engine's equation function instead of a check;
it is listed explicitly in the tool (`functionLevelA` in the registry). (b) = a check citing the item whose nominal or
available strength (or measured value / limit) was passed to `design_examples.assert_printed` — the single
tolerance policy of those tests, 1 % on a printed value — in a test that passed: level `strength`; when
only an intermediate value of the check was compared: level `detail`, which counts as **partial**; one
equation (H1-1a) is reproduced by calling the engine's equation function directly: level `function`,
listed explicitly in the tool. The example ids and pages come from the data files of the tests.

The tier fails, naming the checks, when a used check is `notChecked` / `refused`, or cites an item that is
not fully covered. "Used" follows the rule of the design module (a limit state with a nonzero demand): a
check whose utilisation exceeds 10⁻⁹. The capacity of a check that is not used is not covered by the tier
and is not part of what "verified" covers.

Consequence with the present test suite (see the table in the report): a design reference is not
publishable when it uses, among others, torsional buckling (Eq. E4-1/E4-2: the Design Examples print only
the elastic stress), r_ts computed from Eq. F2-7, web shear with C_v1 < 1 (Eq. G2-3), box sections
(Table B4.1a Case 8, Table B4.1b Case 21), slender flanges in flexure (Eq. F3-2, F6-2…F6-4), the round
HSS local-buckling equations (F8-2…F8-4), the deflection limits (Sec. L1/L2) or the engine's torsion
screening rule.

## 3. Problem library

```
verification/problems/<source-slug>/<problem-id>/
    model.json     a normal benchfea input file
    problem.json   {"id", "title", "tier": "A" | "B",
                    "source": {"work", "edition", "location", "pages", "notes"},
                    "basis": "published" | "derived-here" | "recomputed-from-published-statement",
                    "analysis": text,
                    "targets": [{"path": "/combinations/L/displacements/B/uz"  or  "extract": name, ...args,
                                 "value": number in the model file's units, "unit": text,
                                 "tolerance": {"rel": x} | {"abs": y},
                                 "precisionNote": text, "sign": text}],
                    "status": "active" | "quarantined", "quarantine": {"diagnosis": text},
                    "limitsNote": text}
```

A target is a path into the **results document** (the one `benchfea run` writes; keys separated by `/`,
list items by index; `"document": "design"` selects the design document) or a named extractor:

| extractor | arguments | value |
|---|---|---|
| `bucklingFactor` | `combination`, `mode` (1 = lowest) | positive buckling factor |
| `memberStation` | `combination`, `member`, `quantity` (N, Vy, Vz, T, My, Mz, ux, uy, uz), `x` or `relative`, `side` (`left`/`right` at a point load) | value **at a result station** (no interpolation) |
| `memberExtreme` | `combination`, `member`, `quantity`, `kind` (`max`, `min`, `absmax`) | extreme over the result stations |
| `nodeDisplacement` | `combination`, `node`, `dof` | |
| `reaction` | `combination`, `node`, `component` | force by the support on the structure |
| `sumReactions` | `combination`, `component` | |
| `memberEndForce` | `combination`, `member`, `end`, `component` | force by the joint on the member, local axes |
| `designCheck` | `member`, `check`, `field` (`ratio`, `capacity`, `nominal`, `demand`), `combination` | number of a design check |

`combination` may be omitted when the results hold one. Values are in the units of the model file and in
the engine's sign convention ([CONVENTIONS.md](CONVENTIONS.md)); `sign` records the mapping from the
source's convention.

`verify_problem(dir)` loads the problem, runs `verify_model` on its model (the targets become the tier-A
or tier-B evidence; tiers C, D, E run on the model) and returns a `ProblemRecord` with `outcome`:

* `passed` — declared active, every target within its tolerance and no tier shows a disagreement or an
  error. Whether the model is also cross-checked by two solvers is reported next to it (`crossChecked`,
  model record status): a problem whose model only one solver can represent still reproduces its targets,
  but its model record is `quarantined (not-representable)`.
* `failed` — declared active, but a target is missed or a tier disagrees. The author fixes the problem
  file (a transcription error) or quarantines it; `benchfea verify --all` exits nonzero.
* `quarantined` — declared quarantined, with a written diagnosis (suspected misprint with the evidence,
  approximate method in the source, data read off a figure, another tier failing…). Targets are still
  evaluated and reported; the problem is never counted as passed.
* `invalid` — the directory does not follow the format.

`verify_library()` runs every problem; `tools/build_verification_report.py` writes the report from it.
The problems under `verification/problems/derived/` are tier A; regenerate them with
`python3 verification/derive/derive_problems.py` (`--check` verifies that the stored targets reproduce).

## 4. Unstable models: verifying a diagnosis

For a model the engine refuses as unstable the reference answer is the **diagnosis**
(`MechanismDiagnosis.to_dict()`): the number of independent zero-stiffness motions and what they are. The
record then has `kind = "diagnosis"` and its own tiers; "verified" requires all that apply:

| tier | check |
|---|---|
| N | **Null space of an independently assembled stiffness.** `benchfea.verify.diagnosis.independent_stiffness` builds the stiffness from the model data without any code of `benchfea.solve`: closed-form 12×12 member matrices (the engine inverts a cantilever flexibility), end connections by static condensation of series springs (the engine adds connection flexibilities), its own rigid-offset and constraint kinematics. Checked: the number of zero eigenvalues of the unit-diagonal matrix equals `nMechanisms`; every mode described by a direction or an axis (rigid-body and single-node modes) satisfies Kφ = 0 on that matrix and violates no support or constraint; these modes are linearly independent and, with the count of the "distributed" modes, add up to `nMechanisms`; every node the diagnosis lists moves in the independent null space. |
| K | The same mode vectors satisfy Kφ = 0 on the **engine's** matrix; the eigenvalue count recomputed from it. |
| C | **Foreign solvers under a probing load.** The model's own loading is no test — a mechanism that the loads do not excite is solved without complaint by a solver that does not test for singularity (measured with both). The pipeline therefore replaces the loads by nodal loads that do work on every null vector, so that no equilibrium state exists. A solver agrees when it fails, reports a singular system, returns numbers that are not in equilibrium, or displacements beyond 10³ × the model size; it contradicts the diagnosis when it returns a finite equilibrated solution. Two agreeing solvers are required. |
| E | The diagnosis is identical on a second run and after every design variable is changed. |

Not verifiable (always `quarantined`, category `not-verifiable`, with the diagnosis kept in the record):

* a mechanism that exists only because tension-/compression-only members go slack
  (`stability.slack-mechanism`). Checked: the structure without those members is a mechanism (tiers N, K).
  Not checked by any independent computation: that these members are slack under the loading — a
  statement about a nonlinear problem that has no unique solution;
* a "distributed" mode has no single vector in the record: only the dimension of the null space and the
  participating nodes are compared; a combined rigid-body screw motion is counted, not tested;
* an "ill-conditioned" classification whose eigenvalue lies within a factor 2 of the tolerance: tier N
  fails (the count is not determined); tier C does not apply to ill-conditioned models (they can be
  solved).

The independent matrix uses the engine's documented rule for DOFs that are not part of the solution
(free DOFs of memberless nodes, rotations of nodes joined only by truss-type members), re-implemented —
with one exception: such a DOF that is **loaded** stays in the matrix. A load on a degree of freedom
without stiffness is what the engine refuses with a `no-stiffness` / `only-truss-members` mode of kind
"node", and the motion must then be found independently: as a zero eigenvalue (tier N; the loaded DOFs
come from the load definitions of the first combination that loads one, not from the diagnosis, and
every DOF the diagnosis lists must be among them), as a zero row of the engine's unreduced matrix
counted with its eigenvalues (tier K), and by the foreign solvers, which are given the loaded DOF free
and must fail (tier C; the translators hold only the *unloaded* DOFs without stiffness).

## 5. Tolerances (one policy)

`benchfea.verify.POLICY`, printed in full at the end of the report.

| where | tolerance | relative to |
|---|---|---|
| A, oracle vs engine | 10⁻⁶ | largest value of the same kind (translation, rotation, force, moment) along the member line; the oracle is accurate to about 10⁻⁸ |
| A, targets derived here | relative 10⁻⁶ (absolute for a zero target) | the target |
| B, published targets | see below | the target |
| C | 10⁻⁶ + error bounds of the two results compared (CROSSCHECK.md 1.3); a solver is eligible up to a bound of 5·10⁻³ | model-wide scales of `crosscheck.compare` |
| D | the tolerance stored with each invariant; superposition and reciprocity 10⁻⁹; station statics 10⁻⁹ (linear) / 10⁻⁶ (iterated); station integration 10⁻⁶ | station checks: largest station force / moment / member displacement of the combination |
| E | bitwise / 10⁻⁸ / 10⁻⁶ / 10⁻⁵ as in the table of tier E | model-wide scales |
| diagnosis | eigenvalue ≤ 10⁻⁹ counts as zero; Kφ residual ≤ 2·10⁻⁶ (the record gives directions to 6 decimals) | unit-diagonal matrix; \|K\|\|φ\| |

**Published targets (tier B).** One rule for every problem; authors may not tune it per case:

1. the tolerance is **half a unit in the last digit the source prints**, converted to a relative tolerance
   (`benchfea.verify.printed_precision_tolerance("0.3125") → {"rel": 1.6e-4}`), with the floor 10⁻⁶;
   trailing zeros of an integer are taken as significant (the tighter reading);
2. **or**, where the source itself states an acceptance tolerance, that stated value — e.g. AISC 360-22
   Commentary C2.1 for its benchmark problems (3 % on moments, 5 % on deflections);
3. `precisionNote` records which of the two applies and the printed form of the number; the report shows
   the **actual** difference next to the tolerance;
4. a target that misses is not loosened: the problem is quarantined with a diagnosis.

The loader enforces the form of a tolerance (`{"rel": x}` with x ≥ 10⁻⁶, or `{"abs": y}`), refuses a
relative tolerance on a zero value, and notes a derived-here target declared looser than 10⁻⁶ and a
published target without `precisionNote`; the notes appear in the report.

## 6. Cost

Per model: the engine's solve, two foreign solves per combination (cached by document hash, combination,
solver, options and solver version — `ForeignCache`, in memory or on disk), one re-solve for the design
variables, one for the split, and up to 8 solves per stiffness series (without the buckling analysis).
Typical first-order frames of the model bank take 0.3–1.5 s, second-order ones 1–5 s (report, section 4).
`budget` (seconds) stops tiers C and E from starting new work; what was not run is not passed.
Set `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` (the CLI and the tools do): on small dense matrices a multi-threaded BLAS was
measured two orders of magnitude slower in this project's environment.

## 7. Limits

* Tier A covers a straight member line parallel to a global axis; no frames.
* Tier C needs OpenSeesPy and PyNite installed; without them nothing can be verified. The fixed-end forces
  and the parallel-member decomposition used by both translators are shared code (verified against the
  ODE oracle, CROSSCHECK.md 5): an error there would be common to both foreign routes.
* Models with shear deformation, partial axial / shear / torsion releases, linear buckling results, rigid
  offsets in second order, or rigid links / diaphragms in second order cannot pass tier C and are not
  publishable as verified. Buckling factors are verified only through registered tier-A problems.
* Second order with rigid links or diaphragms: the engine's constraint is a linear kinematic relation;
  a rigid body transmitting forces has a further second-order term (CROSSCHECK.md 3.5; 7.6 % on bank
  model S08). The pipeline does not decide between the two statements: such a combination is not
  cross-checked and its reference is not publishable as verified.
* The foreign error bound of tier C assumes that the refinement sequence keeps contracting at least like
  a first-order method beyond the finest level run; the acceptance table of CROSSCHECK.md 1.3 is the
  evidence, on the kinds of model listed there. A second-order model whose members are close to their
  buckling load has a bound above 5·10⁻³ for OpenSees and is then not cross-checked.
* Tier F credits an item when a check citing it reproduced a printed value; it does not prove that every
  item of that check was exercised by the example (a classification case used on the way to a reproduced
  strength is credited). The hand-calculation side (a) is identified by the naming convention
  `test_hand_*` and by the test code reading the strength or utilisation of the check: that the value
  read is then compared with a number worked by hand is the convention of those tests, not something the
  registry can prove.
* Station values: the internal forces at every result station are recomputed from statics and the
  displacements of the member axis by an integration of the member equations (tier D). Both start from
  values of the results themselves (station forces at the member start, which must equal the end forces
  that tier C cross-checks; deflections of the first and last station, which must equal the node
  displacements carried over the rigid offsets — except at a member end with a released or sprung
  translational connection, where they are taken as stored). In a second-order combination the check
  shows that forces and deflections along a member satisfy the member equations with the stored axial
  force; it does not replace the cross-check of the node results.
* Problems with basis `recomputed-from-published-statement` are not reproductions of published numbers
  (section 2, tier B).
* The record of a verified model states agreement at the tolerances listed — typically 10⁻⁶ for linear
  models and the foreign discretisation error (10⁻⁴…10⁻³) for second-order ones.
